# Proposed evaluation

Before enrolling anyone, ask an independent economics reviewer to check the terminology, numerical fixtures, event timing, omitted costs, and limits of the welfare discussion. Log each issue and how it was resolved. The review is planned.

For a small voluntary adult pilot, use equivalent short pre/post cases (for example, cafe and warehouse, with order counterbalanced). Ask participants to identify a full-cost profit figure, a committed input, the mechanism by which learning or entry matters, and a conditional next decision supported by evidence. Give the post task after using the site. Record usability issues separately from performance. No participant data are collected by this version.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full costs | Omits recognized costs or treats profit as cash | Recognizes costs but misses owner or timing distinction | Reconciles recognized and implicit costs and distinguishes cash flow |
| Time horizons | Uses age or fixed calendar threshold | Names a fixed input without linking it to the decision | Names a relevant fixed input and explains when it can change |
| Competition and knowledge | Assumes one inevitable outcome | Names one mechanism | Distinguishes customer and rival awareness and states conditions for entry |
| Evidence and decision | Unsupported conclusion | Names relevant evidence or a decision threshold | Uses a plausible range and a condition that could change the action |

Specify scoring and analysis before the pilot. Have reviewers score de-identified answers without seeing task timing where feasible, compare agreement and preserve counterexamples. Report sample size, missing responses and item-level changes. A before/after pilot can indicate feasibility and misunderstandings; practice effects and selection prevent a strong causal effectiveness claim.
